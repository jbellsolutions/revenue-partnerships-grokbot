# Social discovery for speaking opportunities

Use social posts as an additional discovery lane, not as proof of a paid booking. Work with available public search/browser access or authorized connected tools. A client does not need SpeakerAgent.ai or a social API subscription. Logged-in/private content requires the client's actual account access; never promise visibility into every platform.

Derive keywords from the client's topics, audiences and industries. Search phrases such as “call for speakers”, “seeking speakers”, “looking for a keynote”, “speaker recommendations”, “panelists wanted”, “webinar guest”, “seminar speaker”, “workshop facilitator”, “paid speaking” and “honorarium”. Add topic, place, year and relevant organizer names. Use public search operators such as `site:linkedin.com/posts`, `site:x.com`, `site:reddit.com`, plus event/association feeds, YouTube descriptions, accessible Facebook event pages and Instagram announcements. Actual site search and date controls differ; inspect the available interface instead of assuming a query syntax.

Read the original post and its date, author, organization, event name, edition, deadline and linked official page/form. Resolve “next Friday” against the post's timestamp and timezone. A fresh repost of an old CFP is still old. Record original URL, post timestamp, observed time and a short relevant excerpt. An indexed snippet is a research lead until inspected; failed access is not a negative search result.

Distinguish: an organizer recruiting a speaker; a speaker advertising availability; a coach selling services; a sponsor selling stage time; an attendee seeking tickets; and an academic paper submission. Only the first is a direct recruitment signal. Others may suggest organizer research, but are not open paid engagements.

Corroborate with the current official event or an identifiable organizer's official post. A small seminar may legitimately have no separate website: keep the authoritative organizer post/event page as its official URL, and support the organizer relationship and route. Verify the same eligibility, compensation, date and route gates used for conferences. Do not reject an external seminar facilitator call merely because the event is a workshop; reject courses that only sell attendance and have no speaking route.

Use `kind: conference` with `event_type: seminar|webinar|workshop|panel|summit|corporate_training|conference` as an agent extension. Keep the exact event/date/organizer identity and preserve all social discovery aliases. Feed qualified candidates into [conference research](../speakeragent-conferences/SKILL.md) and [contact research](../speakeragent-contact-research/SKILL.md).

When access fails, try the organizer site, publicly indexed post, event calendar, newsletter/RSS or another public channel. Preserve the inaccessible URL and uncertainty. Do not solve a blocked platform by inventing a paid API, bypassing login controls, or stopping all other sources. No likes, follows, comments, connection requests, DMs, signups or applications are part of discovery.
