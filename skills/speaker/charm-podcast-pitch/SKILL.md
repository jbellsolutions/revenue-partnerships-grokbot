# Charm Offensive — Podcast Guest Pitch

## Course material (load first)

The approach is adapted from Jon Buchan's Charm Offensive. Its template and playbook files are licensed course material, so they are not in this pack. Load them from `$COURSE_MATERIAL_DIR/charm/` or, failing that, `.secrets/course/charm/` (given at intake): `charm-offensive-foundation/` holds the voice, story and profile files, and each charm skill's own files sit in a folder named after it. If a file is missing, write from the principles on this page and say in your output that the templates weren't given.

Land guest spots by sending a pitch that's genuinely fun to read: an honest ego-rub, a compelling
personal story, and a clear, self-aware case for why you'd be a great guest for *this* show.

## Step 0 — Load what you need

1. The voice and story files from the course folder (`charm-offensive-foundation/`), if given.
2. The user's `charm-profile.md`: their story, their offer, and what an audience gets from them. With no
   story yet, build one with `charm-offensive-foundation` first.
3. The course pitch template (`charm-podcast-pitch/template.md`), if given. Without it, use the shape below.

## Step 1 — Research the show

For each show, find the host's name, the show's angle and audience, a few recent episode topics, and one
true reason it's worth listening to (show site, recent episodes, the host's socials). Compliment only what's
true: if you found the show last week, say you're new to it. Then find one angle for *their* listeners that
recent episodes haven't covered.

## Step 2 — Draft the pitch (our shape)

1. **Subject:** short and specific to the show, never generic.
2. **Opener:** one honest, specific line about the show.
3. **Who you are, as a short story:** where you started, what went wrong, what you worked out, and the result
   that surprises people. Keep it brief enough to read in one scroll.
4. **Why this show:** in a few lines, what the host's listeners would get from the episode, why you can deliver
   it (proof, not adjectives), and the angle nobody has covered on the show yet.
5. **Close:** an easy yes or no for the host, thanks either way, and the user's own sign-off.

Every pitch is written for one show. The story can repeat; the compliment, the angle and the reasons can't.

## Step 3 — Self-check and deliver

Check: the compliment is true; the story is short and has a turn; the reasons are about this show's
listeners; there's one angle they haven't had; nothing is invented. Deliver the subject line (plus one
alternate) and the body. Offer a batch for a list of shows and Gmail drafts, and never send without approval.
